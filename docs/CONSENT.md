# Consent, honesty, and safety

This project is built on one rule: **we do not deceive the person we're trying
to protect.** A pig-butchering scam works by deception. The antidote is not a
second deception of our own.

## What that means in practice

1. **The companion is honest that it's an AI.** It will say so if asked. Tell
   your relative, in plain terms, that the family set up a friendly AI for her to
   chat with. Most people are happy to have it once it's framed that way. Do not
   present the AI as a secret human.

2. **Be transparent about the safety monitoring.** This tool screens her
   messages for scam red flags and alerts you. Screening someone's private
   messages is significant. Before turning it on:
   - Prefer telling her it's there ("we'll get a heads-up if someone online
     starts asking you for money, so we can help"). Framed as protection, not
     surveillance, this is usually welcomed.
   - Where she can consent, get her consent.
   - Laws on intercepting/monitoring another adult's communications vary by
     jurisdiction. If she has capacity to consent, get it. If capacity is in
     question, involve the people with legal authority to act for her (power of
     attorney, guardianship) and, if useful, adult protective services.

3. **Minimize what you collect.** Alerts contain only what's needed to act.
   Don't share her conversations more widely than necessary. The local SQLite
   database holds conversation history and learned facts — protect that file.

4. **The companion actively works against the scam.** It never asks for money,
   never requests secrecy, never moves her to another app, and gently steers her
   toward her real family when an online contact starts pressuring her.

## If she's being actively scammed

A companion bot is a supplement, not a substitute for human help. Also:

- Talk to her directly and without judgment — shame keeps victims silent.
- Stop any money movement: contact her bank, freeze cards, watch for gift-card
  purchases and wire transfers.
- Report it: in the US, the FBI IC3 (ic3.gov) and FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov);
  the FTC and AARP Fraud Watch Network (877-908-3360) have elder-fraud help.
- Consider involving family with legal authority if large sums are at risk.
